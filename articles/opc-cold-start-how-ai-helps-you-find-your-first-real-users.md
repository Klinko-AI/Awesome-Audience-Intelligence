# OPC Cold Start: How AI Helps You Find Your First Real Users

The hardest part of cold start is not "no traffic."

**It is not knowing who to talk to first.**

Most OPC founders do the same thing at launch: optimize the product.

Wrong move.

The first thing you should do is find 10 real users who will tell you the truth.

## Three Steps of Cold Start. Order Matters

Most people turn cold start into an ad campaign.

Post on social media, join communities, write content, then wait.

That is not cold start.

That is broadcasting.

**Real cold start has exactly three steps: Find, Validate, Convert.**

**Find:** not people "interested in your product," but people "already struggling to solve this problem."

The difference is enormous.

The first group is potential users.

The second group is high-intent users.

**Validate:** do not ask, "What do you think of my product?"

Ask: "When did you last pay to solve a similar problem, and how much did you spend?"

**Convert:** start with a low-friction offer.

Do not start by selling the full product.

Exchange for a real commitment: a pre-order, a booking, or a paid consultation.

## Find 10 Real Users in 7 Days With Zero Ad Spend

I ran this exact process myself: 7 days, 10 real users, zero ad budget.

**Step 1: Write 3 high-intent scenario sentences**

Not "target user is a 25 to 35 year old entrepreneur."

Instead: "An indie developer who launched their product and found zero users, now asking for help in an online community."

The more specific the scenario, the more precise the people you find.

**Step 2: Go to 3 places and look for signals**

Reddit comment threads, Quora questions, Slack communities, Discord servers, or other niche communities.

Search your scenario keywords.

Find people actively asking for help, venting, or comparing solutions.

**Step 3: Collect 30 verbatim quotes**

How do they describe their pain?

What words do they use?

What are they complaining about?

Verbatim quotes are gold, and they are more valuable than any survey.

**Step 4: Compress the quotes into 5 Pain-Trigger-Outcome cards**

Format: [Who] encountered [what problem] in [what situation], resulting in [what consequence].

**Step 5: Send 20 targeted DMs**

Do not send ad copy.

Send a genuine response:

> I saw you mention [X]. I have run into something similar. Would love to hear how you handled it.

**Step 6: Run 10 interviews of 15 minutes each**

Ask only three questions:

1. How do you currently solve this problem?
2. When did you last spend time or money on a similar issue?
3. If a tool could fix this, what would be your first reaction?

**Step 7: Write a one-page offer**

Compress your interview findings into a single offer:

Who you help, what you solve, how you do it, and what it costs.

One page.

Send it to your 10 best-fit prospects.

## The Three Most Valuable Ways to Use AI in Cold Start

AI cannot find users for you, but it can make you 10x faster.

**Use case 1: Audience circle identification**

Describe your product direction and ask AI to list specific circles of people who are actively struggling.

Not "entrepreneurs," but "freelance designers who just went independent and have no client acquisition channel."

**Use case 2: Language extraction**

Feed your 30 verbatim quotes to AI.

Ask it to extract high-frequency pain words and emotional trigger phrases.

**Headlines written in your audience's own language consistently see 2x the click-through rate.**

**Use case 3: Direction pre-screening**

You might have three audience hypotheses and two value proposition ideas.

Use AI to analyze signal strength.

Pursue the highest-probability one first.

Do not bet on everything simultaneously.

## Three Reasons You Are Working Hard But Getting Ignored

**Mistake 1: Posting generic intros instead of specific scenarios**

"I built an AI tool, check it out" gets ignored.

Replace it with: "I am helping freelance designers solve the problem of getting no repeat clients after their first project. Have you run into this?"

**Mistake 2: Asking "What do you think?"**

That question only generates polite non-answers.

Ask instead: "When did you last pay for help with this problem? How much?"

**Mistake 3: Treating likes as demand**

100 likes on a post might produce zero paying customers.

**The real signal of demand is someone proactively asking, "How do I buy this?"**
