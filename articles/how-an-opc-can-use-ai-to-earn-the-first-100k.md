# How an OPC Can Use AI to Earn the First ¥100,000

I have seen too many OPCs spend all their time trying to "build a better product."

Three months later, they still do not have a single paying user.

**The real bottleneck is never the product. It is that you do not know who is willing to pay for what you are building.**

## The First ¥100,000 Is Not About Tactics. It Is About Audience Selection

I have studied a group of OPCs that reached their first ¥100,000.

They did not all use the same monetization method.

But they had one thing in common: **each of them found one of these three types of people.**

- **People with real pain:** the problem happens often, and the cost is obvious.
- **People with a payment budget:** they have already paid for similar problems.
- **People with intent to act:** not "maybe later," but "I want to solve this now."

To judge whether an audience is worth betting on, ask three questions:

1. What have they paid for recently?
2. How do they describe the pain of this problem?
3. What workaround are they using right now?

If you cannot answer these three questions, you have not found an audience yet. You have only guessed a direction.

## The Shortest 30-Day Loop From Zero to the First ¥100,000

**Step 1: Choose a specific problem people can pay for**

Not "help founders become more efficient."

A payable problem sounds more like this: "Help independent designers who are already taking client work reduce their customer acquisition time from 10 hours per week to 2."

Only when the problem is specific to an industry, scenario, and outcome does it become something people can pay for.

**Step 2: Build a Problem-Language-Outcome list**

Go to where your target audience gathers: communities, Xiaohongshu, Zhihu, Reddit, Slack groups, or niche forums.

Find 10 verbatim quotes.

Extract the words they use to describe pain, how often they complain, and how they compare existing solutions.

**Step 3: Write 7 pieces of content**

Each piece should match one narrow pain point and include one practical action.

Do not write a generic collection of tips.

Write from a concrete angle: "I went through X, and this is how I solved it."

**Step 4: Productize the content**

Choose one format: a template, checklist, mini-course, or one-on-one consulting.

**Set a low-friction price between ¥99 and ¥499 instead of trying to sell a big course immediately.**

**Step 5: Use 20 conversations to close the first batch**

Reach out actively through comment replies, direct messages, and relevant communities.

This is not advertising.

Use the scenario in your content to create resonance, then move interested people into a private conversation.

## Three Traps You Will Probably Fall Into

**Trap 1: Using a persona instead of payment signals**

"25 to 35 years old, likes trying new things" is not an audience.

It is a guess.

Fix it by finding their three most recent payments for similar problems, even if those records come from interviews or survey answers.

**Trap 2: Confusing content traffic with content conversion**

High views do not equal conversion.

**Every piece of content needs a clear action: message me, join the waitlist, or fill out a form.**

Fix it by adding a clear next step at the end, instead of ending with "share this if you found it useful."

**Trap 3: Replacing what users say with what you think they need**

You may think users need an "all-in-one AI assistant."

They may actually be saying, "I do not know how to quickly process customer feedback."

Fix it by using their own words in your headlines and selling points, not your summarized version.

## Use AI to Make Audience Selection 10x Faster

The most valuable use of AI is turning "judging audiences by instinct" into "screening directions with evidence."

**Audience circle identification:** Enter your product direction and ask AI to list specific circles of people who are struggling frequently, instead of broad demographic groups.

**Native language extraction:** Give AI your collected user quotes and ask it to extract high-frequency words, emotional triggers, and hidden needs.

**Monetization direction pre-screening:** For the same product, compare three audience directions and let AI help analyze which direction has the strongest signals and the easiest path to conversion.
